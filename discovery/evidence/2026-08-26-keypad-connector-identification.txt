loa keypad connector: measurements, and three wrong answers on the way
======================================================================

  Bench session 2026-08-26, Matt measuring, phone open. Nothing dismantled.

THE PHONE AND THE BOARD
-----------------------
  Bittel HA41T-25, a wall-mounted corded set from Bittel's 41 series
  (hotel bathroom / communal). Keypad daughterboard silkscreened:

      HA(41)T-25   KB   T1   2020.1.15

  KB is the keypad board, T1 a revision, built 15 January 2020. The connector
  is silkscreened PX.

  TWELVE KEYS, NOTHING ELSE. No redial, no flash, no guest-service keys. The
  41 series is the plainest set in Bittel's range, which is consistent. The
  38 and 48 series advertise up to 10 programmable service keys and would have
  broken the seven-conductor assumption immediately.

MEASURED
--------
  header on KB board     7 pins, vertical, standing straight up
  cable                  grey flat ribbon, ONE RED CONDUCTOR at the edge
  ribbon width           9.0 mm
  conductors             7, 28 AWG
  termination            MASS TERMINATED. The ribbon enters the housing
                         intact; conductors are not fanned out and crimped.
  cable housing width    11.5 mm
  retention              NO LATCH. Small plastic rails on the cable-side
                         housing engage side slots on the board-side part.
                         Friction and keying, not a positive lock.

DERIVED: 1.27 mm PITCH
----------------------
  From the ribbon width, since a mass-terminated ribbon's pitch MUST equal the
  connector's:

      pitch   predicts ribbon   error vs 9.0 mm measured
      1.00        7.00 mm            22.2%
      1.25        8.75 mm             2.8%
      1.27        8.89 mm             1.2%   <-- best fit
      1.50       10.50 mm            16.7%

  1.27 mm is 0.05 inch, the worldwide ribbon standard, and 28 AWG is its
  standard conductor. Every other observation now agrees rather than
  half-agreeing:

      ribbon 9.0 mm        1.2% from prediction
      "about 1.2 mm" gap   1.27 rounds to that
      JST GH "too small"   1.25 vs 1.27 differs by 0.12 mm across the whole
                           span, invisible by eye, so no conflict either way
      housing 11.5 mm      1.94 mm wall per side, right for an IDC clamp with
                           strain relief
      no latch, rails      consistent with a low-profile IDT receptacle

CANDIDATE, NOT CONFIRMED: MOLEX PICOFLEX
----------------------------------------
  Picoflex is a 1.27 mm IDT system built for standard ribbon, specifying
  26-28 AWG, circuit sizes 4 to 26, low-profile vertical header to a
  ribbon-terminated receptacle, available WITH OR WITHOUT LATCHES. Every one
  of those matches.

  IT IS A FIT, NOT AN IDENTIFICATION. Other vendors make 1.27 mm ribbon IDT
  connectors. Nothing on this housing has been read as a Molex marking. To
  confirm: look for a logo or moulded part number on the receptacle, or buy
  ONE 7-circuit part and test-fit before ordering a quantity.

THREE ANSWERS I GOT WRONG BEFORE THIS ONE
-----------------------------------------
  Recorded so they are not re-litigated, and because the pattern is the point.

  1. "1.25 mm FFC." Derived from the 11.5 mm housing using an FFC's ~2 mm wall
     allowance. Wrong because it was not an FFC at all: no exposed fingers.
     The wall allowance came from the assumed family, and it took the answer
     with it.
  2. "1.5 mm, JST ZH." Same housing width, re-derived with wire-to-board wall
     thickness once the header was known to be pins. Wrong because the part is
     ribbon-terminated, and ZH is crimp-only with no ribbon variant.
  3. "1.5 mm rules out anything smaller, per the JST GH comparison." Wrong
     because standard ribbon is 1.27 and GH is 1.25. A 0.12 mm difference over
     the full span cannot be seen, so that comparison never carried the weight
     put on it.

  Also worth recording: the first pitch table offered contained only JST and
  Molex families, so it silently excluded TE entirely. Matt asked whether it
  could be an AMP CT, and AMP CT spans 1.2 / 1.5 / 2.0 mm. The candidate set
  was too small and the answer looked better determined than it was.

  THE MEASUREMENT THAT SETTLED IT was the cheapest one available: the width of
  the ribbon, with a ruler. Every earlier attempt inferred pitch from a housing
  dimension through an assumed wall thickness, which is an assumption stacked
  on an assumption. Conventions rule 10 again.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE BUILD
-----------------------------
  1.27 mm ribbon is exactly half of J11's 0.1 inch IDC pitch, and 0.05 inch
  ribbon is what 2.54 mm IDC connectors are built to bite. The far end is a
  solved problem.

  The safest path needs nothing ordered: clip the ribbon at the mainboard end,
  keep the red conductor as pin 1, and beep out the twelve keys from the
  stripped ends. The phone's original mainboard is replaced by caryatid, so
  that harness is scrap. The keypad and its header are never touched.

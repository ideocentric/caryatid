  Red/green common-anode bicolour 5 mm LED, Amazon B01CFZMO3I, for J4.
  NO BOARD INVOLVED. Supply, resistor, meter.

  !! THIS MEASUREMENT IS THE ONLY INFORMATION THAT EXISTS ABOUT THIS

     from the vendor: not the forward voltages, not the package, not

INSTRUMENTS                                 (see bench-instruments.yaml)
  Meter    Fluke 101, DC V and diode test
  Supply   Jesverty, 5.00 V then 3.00 V
  R        nominal 1k, MEASURED 990 ohm, used at that value

A7.2  COMMON ANODE
------------------
  Diode test. RED PROBE ON THE COMMON PIN conducts to both other pins.
  => COMMON ANODE. PASS. This is what the board needs; a common cathode
     would have required a high-side switch.

  🔴 A SECOND RESULT HIDES IN THIS ONE. The diode test LIT THE GREEN
     DIE. A handheld's diode test supplies only ~2.4 V open circuit, so
     it CANNOT light a 3.0-3.2 V InGaN green. That it worked at all is
     independent proof the green is a low-Vf die, arrived at before any
     voltage was read. Two methods, same answer.

A7.3  FORWARD VOLTAGES, AND THE CHEMISTRY QUESTION
---------------------------------------------------
  Rig:  supply --> 990 ohm --> LED --> 0 V,  Fluke across the LED.

  supply    green Vf    green I     red Vf    red I
  -------   --------    --------    ------    -------
  5.00 V     2.322 V    2.71 mA     1.862 V   3.17 mA
  3.00 V     2.234 V    0.77 mA     1.810 V   1.20 mA

  >>> GREEN IS AlGaInP. PASS.

  2.322 V is nowhere near InGaN true green at 3.0-3.2 V: 0.7 V of
  separation. The reading is if anything biased LOW, since vendor Vf
  figures are quoted at 20 mA and this is 2.7 mA, and scaling up would
  not approach 3.0 V.

  Both dies shift 120-160 mV per decade of current, which is ordinary
  diode behaviour. Nothing anomalous in either.

  WHY THIS WAS THE WHOLE QUESTION: J4 hangs on VOUT, the cell, roughly
  4.2 V falling to 3.0 V, not a regulated rail. sourcing.md put it
  plainly: "if this bicolour uses the same die as the RGB it is the
  wrong part for J4, however good it looks on the bench at full
  charge." It does not. The part survives.

THE FLAT-CELL ACCEPTANCE TEST
------------------------------
  Green lights at 3.00 V, drawing 0.77 mA. PASS.
  Predicted 0.68 mA from the 5 V forward voltage; the real Vf falls at
  lower current, leaving more across the resistor. Reality is slightly
  better than the prediction.

  ⚠️ STILL A VISUAL JUDGEMENT OUTSTANDING: whether 0.77 mA is actually
     READABLE on a flat cell. The meter proves conduction, not
     legibility, and the requirement is that the charge state can be
     SEEN. Not yet confirmed by eye.

ON THE BOARD, with R9/R10 at 1 kohm from VOUT
----------------------------------------------
  cell      red        green
  4.2 V     2.33 mA    1.86 mA
  3.0 V     1.19 mA    0.77 mA

  CORRECTION TO indicators.md: it says "1 kohm from 4.2 V through
  ~2.1 V is 2.1 mA per die". With the measured 2.322 V it is 1.88 mA,
  about 10% optimistic. The amber total of 4.2 mA survives by accident,
  since red's surplus covers green's shortfall.

🔴 NEW: THE AMBER DRIFTS TOWARD RED AS THE CELL DRAINS
-------------------------------------------------------
  cell      red : green
  4.2 V        1.25
  3.0 V        1.55        <-- 24% more red-dominated

  Green's higher forward voltage eats proportionally more of a
  SHRINKING headroom, so green fades 2.4x across the cell's life while
  red fades only 2.0x.

  TWO CONSEQUENCES, NEITHER PREVIOUSLY WRITTEN DOWN:

  1. TUNING R9/R10 AT FULL CHARGE DOES NOT HOLD AT FLAT CELL.
     indicators.md anticipated trimming the balance but treated it as
     one fixed adjustment. It is not fixed, it MOVES with state of
     charge. Trim at mid-cell, not on a freshly charged pack.

  2. RESISTORS CANNOT FULLY FIX IT. The drift is driven by the Vf
     DIFFERENCE against a FALLING RAIL, not by the resistor ratio. R9
     and R10 can centre the balance; they cannot stop it moving.

  IT THREATENS NOTHING. Amber going more orange as the battery empties
  is harmless and arguably reads correctly. Recorded so it is not later
  discovered and mistaken for a fault.

  The trim direction is also now quantified rather than guessed: red
  draws 24% more current than green through equal resistors, ON TOP OF
  red being the brighter die per milliamp. R9 wants RAISING, around
  1.2k to equalise current, and further by eye since equal current is
  not equal brightness.

NOT YET DONE ON THIS PART

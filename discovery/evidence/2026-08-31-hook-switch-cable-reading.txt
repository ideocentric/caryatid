loa hook switch: a reading taken through the phone's own board
==============================================================

  Bench session 2026-08-31, Matt measuring. Same session as the keypad
  beep-out; separate subject, separate record: `loa-hook-switch`.

MEASURED
--------
  Probed: the CABLE running from the off-hook switch on the phone's LARGE
  BOARD to a separate board. NOT the switch contacts.

      off hook (handset lifted)    27 kohm
      on hook  (handset resting)   37 Mohm

WHAT IT HAS TO DRIVE
--------------------
  caryatid SW3, per ADR 0007:

      3V3 --[10k pull-up]-- NODE --[10k series]-- 74HC14 in
                              |                       |
                            switch                  C to GND (1 uF, hook lever)
                              |
                             GND

  A CMOS input draws no current, so nothing flows in the series 10k and the
  node sits at a plain divider between the pull-up and the switch:

      Vnode = 3.3 * Rsw / (10k + Rsw)

      Rsw         Vnode
      ---------   -------
              0    0.00 V   ideal metal contact
            100    0.03 V
          1,000    0.30 V
          3,200    0.80 V   edge of comfortable
          4,350    1.00 V   edge of guaranteed
         10,000    1.65 V
         27,000    2.41 V   <- MEASURED
     37,000,000    3.30 V   on hook, measured

  74HC14 thresholds at 3V3. INTERPOLATED: the datasheet gives 2.0 / 4.5 / 6.0 V
  and not 3.3 V.
      VT+   typical ~1.7 V   worst case up to ~2.3 V
      VT-   typical ~1.0 V   worst case down to ~0.7 V

AS MEASURED, IT FAILS
---------------------
  2.41 V is above even the WORST-CASE VT+. The Schmitt input never leaves logic
  high and the hook never registers as closed. Not marginal, not a debounce
  question: about 8x too high.

  It would present as a completely dead hook switch on an instrument where
  everything else works, which is the kind of fault that gets blamed on
  firmware for a day before anyone measures the input.

  To pass, the switch must be under:
      2.69 kohm   for 0.7 V, the guaranteed worst-case VT-
      4.35 kohm   for 1.0 V, typical

BUT THIS IS ALMOST CERTAINLY NOT THE SWITCH
-------------------------------------------
  Two things say the 27 kohm belongs to the phone's circuitry and not to a
  pair of contacts:

  1. A MECHANICAL HOOK LEVER IS METAL. Closed, it should read essentially
     zero. Nothing about a lever and a contact produces 27 kohm.
  2. ON HOOK READ 37 Mohm, NOT OPEN CIRCUIT. Genuinely open contacts read OL.
     A finite tens-of-megohm figure is leakage through a semiconductor or
     across a board. Something is in the path.

  Probed through a cable into a separate board, the reading is the switch in
  series or parallel with whatever that board puts across it.

  THIS IS THE THIRD TIME THIS SHAPE HAS APPEARED IN THIS PROJECT:
      - hot-melt glue on the handset capsule, hiding the DC resistance
      - the continuity beeper on the keypad, which would have hidden a healthy
        key behind silence
      - and now a hook switch measured through the board it is wired to
  Each time the instrument or the setup reported itself rather than the part.

AND IT MAY NOT MATTER AT ALL
----------------------------
  LOA REPLACES THE PHONE'S MAINBOARD. Whatever network produced 27 kohm is
  scrap. caryatid wires to the switch contacts directly, which is exactly how
  the keypad was reached: at its own header, not through the mainboard. If the
  contacts are metal, this closes at zero cost.

NEXT MEASUREMENT
----------------
  THE SWITCH CONTACTS THEMSELVES. Cable disconnected at BOTH ends, probe the
  switch terminals on the large board.

      expect      near zero ohm off hook, OL on hook
      that gives  clears acceptance by two orders of magnitude, record closes

  IF IT STILL READS TENS OF KOHM it is not a plain mechanical switch. Check
  what it is before redesigning anything: an optical interrupter is exactly
  this shape, a phototransistor conducting at tens of kohm; so is a Hall
  sensor; so is a plain switch with a resistor beside it on the same board.

  ALSO RECORD WHICH WAY ROUND. Off hook is the handset LIFTED. Note whether
  the contacts close on lift or on rest. ADR 0007's polarity argument depends
  on it: switch-open must be the safe state, which for loa is on hook, silent,
  mic bias off. Getting this backwards means a broken lead leaves the
  instrument live with the mic hot, which is the outcome the ADR chose the
  inverted polarity specifically to avoid.

NOT A FIX YET, and listed only so it is not reinvented under pressure:
  - raising the pull-up above 100 kohm would put 27 kohm under 0.7 V, but it
    slows the RC and raises noise pickup on an unshielded panel lead, which is
    the exact thing ADR 0007 chose the RC to suppress
  - a plain microswitch on the hook lever makes the input metal again and is
    cheaper and more robust than any of it
  None of this is actionable until the contacts are measured.

ALSO RECORDED THIS SESSION
--------------------------
  THE PHONE HAS FOUR BOARDS, which was not written down anywhere before:
      1. keypad board, HA(41)T-25 KB T1, connector PX
      2. large board, carries the off-hook switch
      3. a separate board the off-hook cable runs to
      4. a fourth, not yet characterised
  Only the keypad and the hook switch are wanted. The rest is line circuit and
  is replaced by caryatid.

===========================================================================
CORRECTION, same session: the numbers are withdrawn, the POLARITY stands
===========================================================================

  Matt: "the readings can be off due to the challenge of the connection to
  take the reading. What it does indicate is that the telephone spring switch
  is normally closed and when pressed it's open."

WHAT IS WITHDRAWN
-----------------
  27 kohm and 37 Mohm are NOT usable figures. The probe connection was awkward
  and the contact was poor. Nothing above that reasons numerically from 27
  kohm describes the part; it describes the probing.

  ONE THING SURVIVES, AND IT IS LOGIC NOT PRECISION. A poor probe connection
  can only ADD series resistance. It cannot subtract any. So:

      27 kohm is an UPPER BOUND on the true closed resistance.

  The real figure is at or below it, possibly far below. That does not clear
  the 2.69 kohm threshold, but it does mean the failure analysis above is the
  worst conceivable case rather than the likely one. The spring contacts of a
  telephone hook are metal on metal and would normally read well under an ohm.

WHAT STANDS, AND IT IS THE MORE USEFUL HALF
-------------------------------------------
  THE HOOK IS A SPRING SWITCH, NORMALLY CLOSED. Pressed, it opens. The resting
  handset is what presses it.

      handset ON hook  (resting, spring pressed)   ->  switch OPEN
      handset OFF hook (lifted,  spring released)  ->  switch CLOSED

  The two readings corroborate this DIRECTIONALLY even though neither value is
  trustworthy: off hook read low, on hook read effectively open. Direction
  survives bad probe contact in a way magnitude does not. This is why the
  polarity is the keeper and the ohms are not.

IT CONFIRMS ADR 0007 RATHER THAN THREATENING IT
-----------------------------------------------
  ADR 0007 argues the inverted output is the SAFE polarity: "an unplugged cable
  reads identically to switch open, for loa that means on-hook, so a broken
  lead leaves the instrument silent with the mic bias off."

  THAT ARGUMENT RESTED ON AN ASSUMPTION IT NEVER STATED: that on-hook is the
  open state. Nobody had checked. It is now measured, and it holds.

      on hook   spring pressed  switch OPEN    node -> 3V3
                74HC14 in HIGH  D7 LOW         silent, mic bias off
      off hook  spring free     switch CLOSED  node -> GND
                74HC14 in LOW   D7 HIGH        live
      broken lead               looks OPEN     D7 LOW    silent

  Firmware reads D7 HIGH as off-hook. 12-phone-build.md's "switch closed is
  high" is correct as written and needs no change.

A BONUS FOR A BATTERY INSTRUMENT
--------------------------------
  ADR 0007 warns that each CLOSED switch draws 330 uA through its pull-up and
  that this "is not zero on a build with latching switches that sit closed".
  LOA'S HOOK SITS OPEN AT REST, because at rest the handset is down. The 330 uA
  flows only while the instrument is in use. Idle draw on this channel is nil,
  and the ADR's concern does not apply to it.

WHAT IS STILL WANTED, NARROWED TO ONE NUMBER
--------------------------------------------
  THE CLOSED RESISTANCE OF THE SPRING CONTACTS, to usable precision. Cable off
  at both ends, probed at the switch terminals, HANDSET LIFTED since that is
  the closed state.

  GET A GOOD CONTACT THIS TIME. Clip rather than hold. Short the probes first
  and read the meter's own lead resistance, so the figure is the switch and not
  the leads. At a 2.69 kohm threshold a couple of ohms of lead is irrelevant;
  tens of kilohm of bad contact is precisely what went wrong.

  DO NOT RE-MEASURE THE POLARITY. It is settled and it answered in the design's
  favour.

POLARITY IN CONFLICT. Raised within the hour of being recorded confirmed.
===========================================================================

  Two statements from Matt, same day, and they are opposites:

    A, earlier   "the telephone spring switch is normally closed and when
                  pressed it's open."   Handset down presses it.
                  => ON HOOK = OPEN,  OFF HOOK = CLOSED

    B, later     "When the hand set is down (on Hook), the phone switch is
                  engaged connecting the switch."
                  => ON HOOK = CLOSED, OFF HOOK = OPEN

  The section above this one recorded A as confirmed. THAT WAS PREMATURE and
  it is withdrawn. Both are on the record; neither is fact.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE LEANS TOWARD, WITHOUT SETTLING IT
---------------------------------------------------
  The measurements support A: off hook read 27 kohm (conductive), on hook read
  37 Mohm (effectively open). But those readings were themselves withdrawn for
  poor probe contact, and A PROBE THAT SLIPPED ON THE ON-HOOK READING WOULD
  PRODUCE EXACTLY 37 Mohm FROM A CLOSED SWITCH. So the lean is real and it is
  not proof.

B MAY NOT BE A CONTRADICTION AT ALL
-----------------------------------
  "Engaged" is ambiguous, and the ambiguity is worth naming rather than
  guessing past. It reads naturally as the MECHANICAL sense: the resting
  handset engaging the plunger. That is true under BOTH A and B and says
  nothing about the circuit. "Connecting the switch" then reads as electrical.

  If B meant only that the handset engages the mechanism, A stands untouched
  and there is no conflict. If B meant the contacts close, A is wrong. One
  sentence from Matt settles which, and no measurement is needed for that half.

THE PART LINK
-------------
  An Amazon listing for a generic telephone hook switch, B0CP269PT2, was
  supplied. NOT FETCHED: the request returned HTTP 500.

  It would have been weak evidence regardless. A visually similar aftermarket
  part is not the part in this phone, and marketplace listings rarely state
  contact configuration reliably. Recorded as a pointer, not as a source.

WHY THIS IS NOT A DETAIL
------------------------
  THE FAIL-SAFE ARGUMENT INVERTS WITH IT. ADR 0007 chose the inverted output
  because a broken lead reads as switch-open, which it assumed meant on-hook
  and therefore silent:

    IF A   broken lead -> open -> ON HOOK  -> silent, mic bias off.  SAFE
    IF B   broken lead -> open -> OFF HOOK -> LIVE, MIC HOT

  B is precisely the failure ADR 0007 states it chose this polarity to avoid.
  Under B the ADR's rationale does not hold for loa and something must change.

THE DECISIVE TEST, ten seconds once the meter is on the contacts
----------------------------------------------------------------
  Cable off at BOTH ends. Probes CLIPPED to the switch terminals. Meter in
  resistance. PRESS THE PLUNGER BY HAND, which is what the resting handset
  does, and WATCH THE METER THROUGH THE TRANSITION.

      pressed reads LOW    -> B. The fail-safe inverts.
      pressed reads OPEN   -> A. ADR 0007 stands as written.

  Watch it through the transition rather than taking two static readings. A
  slipped probe then shows up as noise instead of as an answer, which is
  exactly how the first set of numbers went wrong.

AND COUNT THE TERMINALS. It decides whether B is a problem or a non-event.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
      TWO    SPST. You get the polarity the switch gives you. If it is B, the
             fix has to happen in the harness or in firmware, and firmware
             cannot fix the broken-lead case.
      THREE  SPDT: common, normally-open, normally-closed. The polarity becomes
             a WIRING CHOICE rather than a constraint. Pick the contact that
             makes on-hook open and ADR 0007's fail-safe holds for free.

  Telephone hook switches are commonly SPDT, because the set itself needs both
  senses. CHECK THE TERMINAL COUNT BEFORE TREATING B AS A DESIGN PROBLEM.

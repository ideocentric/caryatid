BEFORE PROBING
--------------
  [ ] Phone unplugged from the wall line. 48 V DC, ~90 V AC on ring.
  [ ] Cable disconnected at the MAINBOARD end. Keypad end stays connected.
      Still plugged in means measuring through the DTMF chip's pull-ups and
      every reading is a phantom path.
  [ ] Meter in RESISTANCE mode, autorange or 200 kohm.
      DO NOT USE THE CONTINUITY BEEPER. Conductive rubber on carbon closes at
      hundreds of ohms to a few kohm. A beeper triggering below ~50 ohm stays
      silent on a healthy key and the wrong conclusion is "dead keypad".
  [ ] Alligator-clip leads on both probes, so both hands are free to press.
  [ ] If the free end is a socket housing, seat a header pin or a short piece
      of solid-core wire in each socket to give the clip something to grip.
      Do not pierce insulation.

---------------------------------------
  Hold key `1` down. Clip probe A to BLACK, sweep B across the other six.
  No hit, move A to RED and sweep the remaining five. And so on. 21 probes
  worst case, and only this once.

      key 1 closes on:  .......... and ..........   at .......... ohm


STEP 3. EXPLOIT THE GRID
------------------------
  Every remaining key shares a conductor with one already found. Key `2` is
  in key `1`'s row, key `4` is in key `1`'s column. Clip A to one of key 1's
  two conductors and sweep: a handful of probes each, not 21.

  Faster still, three keys at a time: `1` `2` `3` share ONE row conductor.
  `1` `4` `7` `*` share ONE column conductor. Confirm a group in one clip
  position rather than key by key.

!! 15 OHM CONTRADICTS THE RECORD'S PREMISE
------------------------------------------
  `loa-keypad-matrix` warns at length that telephone keypads are conductive
  rubber pills on carbon pads, closing at hundreds of ohms to a few kohm, and
  that a continuity beeper triggering below ~50 ohm would stay silent on a
  healthy key. That is NOT this keypad. 15 ohm is a metallic closure, almost
  certainly a metal dome or tact switch, which is consistent with a 2020-dated
  hotel set. The beeper would in fact have worked here.

  The precaution was still correct to take: it cost one meter setting and it
  was not knowable in advance. But the record should stop asserting the carbon
  premise as though it described this part.

  Consequence for the design, and it is a good one: 15 ohm against the Seed's
  ~40 kohm internal pull-up, through J11's 100 ohm series, is far below the
  5 kohm "comfortable" threshold. No external pull-down, no stronger drive.

  Caveat to close: 15 ohm has not been corrected for lead and clip resistance.
  Short the probes together and read it once. Immaterial to the verdict at this
  margin, but it is the difference between a measured figure and a raw one.

  Series diodes are ruled out on these three paths by the value alone. A diode
  in series cannot read 15 ohm in resistance mode. Confirm on one key by
  reversing the probes rather than assuming it generalises.

WIRING CONSEQUENCE, WORTH DECIDING BEFORE THE HARNESS IS MADE OFF
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  Harness order at the connector is BLK RED WHT YEL ORG GRN BLU. Rows are the
  first four IN KEYPAD ORDER. Columns are the last three IN REVERSE: position
  5 is ORG which is column 3, position 7 is BLU which is column 1.

  So a straight pin-for-pin harness onto J11's D0-D6 gives rows D0-D3 in

    - keep the harness straight and reverse the column index in the scan
  Either is fine. Choosing neither, and discovering it when 1 reads as 3, is
  the outcome to avoid.

THE CONNECTOR CONFLICT, RESOLVED. And the 1.27 mm answer was wrong.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  PROVENANCE, answered: the seven coloured wires are a JST ZH 7-way STUB
  CABLE, bought to test with. Not the phone's cable. So the 2026-08-26 block
  describes the original correctly; what it got wrong is the conclusion.

  IT MATES, AND IT CONDUCTED ON ALL SEVEN. The whole beep-out above was made
  through that stub. Twelve distinct pairs, every conductor carrying, 10.6 to
  17.5 ohm. That is the proof, and it is stronger than "it seemed to fit":

      a 1.27 mm plug on a 1.5 mm header is 0.23 mm out per position
      by position 7 it is 1.38 mm out, near a whole pitch
      the outer contacts cannot make contact at all

  Clean readings on all seven are only possible at the correct pitch. The
  matrix measurement WAS the connector test, and neither of us noticed.

WHY 1.27 mm SURVIVED AS LONG AS IT DID
--------------------------------------
  7-way connector, the two candidates:

      part        pitch   pin1-pin7 span   ribbon width   housing approx
      --------    -----   --------------   ------------   --------------
      JST ZH      1.50          9.00           10.50           11.50
      Picoflex    1.27          7.62            8.89            9.89

      measured 2026-08-26:      9.0 "ribbon width"       11.5 housing

  THE 9.0 FIGURE NEVER DISCRIMINATED. Read as overall ribbon width it gives
  1.27 mm at a 1.2% fit. Read as pin-centre span it gives 1.50 mm EXACTLY.
  One number, two readings, both landing on 9.0, and the datum, where the
  ruler was actually laid, was never written down. The derivation then
  presented 1.2% as though it excluded 1.5 mm. It never computed 1.5 mm under
  the other reading.

  Worse: the 11.5 mm housing width DOES discriminate. ZH fits it (10.5 mm of
  pitch territory plus ~0.5 mm wall each side). Picoflex misses by 1.6 mm.
  That figure was set aside as the inferior method, in favour of the one that
  could not tell the two apart. The FIRST answer, "1.5 mm, JST ZH", derived
  from the housing on 2026-08-26, was right and was argued away.

  THE LESSON, and this record already half contained it. Filed under
  `what_settled_it` is "Measure the thing whose dimension is defined by what
  you want to know". That was followed and still produced the wrong answer.
  9.0 mm is not a measurement. "9.0 mm, outer conductor edge to outer
  conductor edge" is. RECORD THE DATUM, NOT ONLY THE NUMBER.

TWO CONSEQUENCES THAT BITE LATER IF NOT WRITTEN DOWN NOW
-------------------------------------------------------
  1. THE COLOUR MAP IS EPHEMERAL. Black-red-white-yellow-orange-green-blue is
     one vendor's rainbow order on a bought stub. It is not a property of the
     keypad. Swap the cable and every colour in the map above means nothing.
     The durable map is by header pin:

         pin 1 -> row 1        pin 5 -> column 3
         pin 2 -> row 2        pin 6 -> column 2
         pin 3 -> row 3        pin 7 -> column 1
         pin 4 -> row 4

     Rows in keypad order, columns backwards.

  2. !! PIN 1 IS NOT ANCHORED. The table above assumes the "left to right"
     used when reading the wire colours begins at header pin 1. Nothing ties
     that to a physical feature: not the PX silkscreen, not a housing key, not
     a board edge. AND THE ERROR IS SILENT IF IT IS WRONG. Flipped, rows are
     still rows and columns are still columns, the matrix still maps perfectly,
     and key 1 reads as key #.

HOW THE PHONE SHIPPED: the original ribbon's red conductor is on PIN 7
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Matt, 2026-08-31. The original grey ribbon has its RED CONDUCTOR AT PIN 7,
  not pin 1.

  THIS REFUTES A LINE IN THE 2026-08-26 RECORD. That session wrote "one RED
  CONDUCTOR at the edge (pin 1 marker)". The observation was that red is at an
  edge, which is true. "Pin 1 marker" was the standard ribbon convention
  assumed on top of it, and it is wrong for this cable. The convention was
  never stated as an assumption, so it read as though it had been observed.

  READ WITH THE SAME LEFT-TO-RIGHT as the ZH stub, the original red sits at
  the opposite end from the stub's BLACK. So on the original cable:

      red conductor  ->  pin 7  ->  COLUMN 1  ->  keys 1 / 4 / 7 / *

  WHY IT MATTERS. Anyone reusing the original ribbon and trusting "red is pin
  1" installs it reversed, and hits precisely the silent failure described
  above: rows still read as rows, columns still as columns, the matrix maps
  perfectly, and key 1 comes up as key #. This note is the difference between
  that and a working harness.

  IT DOES NOT FULLY CLOSE THE ANCHORING QUESTION. Both cables were read with
  the same unanchored left-to-right, so this fixes the two cables relative to
  EACH OTHER. Neither is yet fixed to the board. Still owed: which header pin
  BLACK sits on, named against a physical feature that survives unplugging.

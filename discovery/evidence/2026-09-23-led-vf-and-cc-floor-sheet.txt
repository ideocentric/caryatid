LED FORWARD VOLTAGE AT RATED CURRENT, AND THE SUPPLY'S CC FLOOR
================================================================

  2026-09-23. BLANK. Nothing below is a result yet.

  NO BOARD IS INVOLVED. Nothing is powered but the part under test.

WHY THESE TWO TESTS EXIST
-------------------------
  TEST 1. A green die measured 2.54-2.59 V with the supply in CC at a set
  2 mA. The same die measured 2.454 V at 2.57 mA under constant voltage.
  VOLTAGE WENT UP AS CURRENT WENT DOWN, which a diode cannot do. Read
  against the curve those CV points draw, 2.54-2.59 V corresponds to
  somewhere between 4 and 10 mA depending on series resistance, i.e. TWO
  TO FIVE TIMES what the supply was displaying.

  `bench-instruments` has carried the supply's MINIMUM SETTABLE CURRENT
  LIMIT as NOT_YET_VERIFIED since it arrived, and a 0-5 A instrument asked
  to hold 2 mA is regulating at 0.04% of full scale. This test closes that.
  IT MATTERS MORE THAN TEST 2: it bears on every current-controlled
  measurement this project will ever take.

  The competing explanation is a DIFFERENT UNIT from the 100-piece bag,
  where +/-0.13 V is unremarkable. The two are not exclusive.

  TEST 2. Both bags are labelled 3.0-3.2 V green. On 2026-09-22 a claim
  was written that the measured forward voltages disproved that, and it
  was WITHDRAWN on 2026-09-23: it extrapolated a log-only diode fit from
  low-current points out to 20 mA, omitting the series resistance that
  dominates there. Low-current readings CANNOT separate the two
  chemistries. 20 mA, the label's own condition, can.

  🔴 NOTHING GATES ON TEST 2. J12 runs from 5 V with headroom either way,
  and J4 was settled FUNCTIONALLY by measuring its operating point
  (0.77 mA at a 3.00 V supply, green lights). This is closing a loose
  claim, not qualifying a part.

🔴 THE WIRING DETAIL THAT WOULD QUIETLY RUIN EVERY READING
-----------------------------------------------------------
  BOTH PARTS ARE COMMON ANODE. The common pin goes UP to node B. The die
  being tested puts its own cathode DOWN to node C.

  LEAVE EVERY OTHER CATHODE COMPLETELY DISCONNECTED.

      RGB, 4 pin        node B ---- COMMON ANODE
                        node C ---- green cathode
                        float  ---- red cathode     <-- NOT connected
                        float  ---- blue cathode    <-- NOT connected

      bicolour, 3 pin   node B ---- COMMON ANODE
                        node C ---- green cathode
                        float  ---- red cathode     <-- NOT connected

  If a second cathode is also tied to node C, that die lights too and
  SPLITS THE CURRENT. Your "20 mA into green" becomes something else with
  NO VISIBLE SIGN THAT ANYTHING IS WRONG. This is the failure that looks
  like a result.

      Recorded 2026-09-20 as 10.2 ohm.
                                            shunt = ............ ohm

  1A  Fluke on DC mV, probes A to B, across the shunt.
                                            reading = ............ mV
                                     real current = mV / shunt

  1B  Fluke on DC V, probes B to C, across the LED.

                  => a permanent constraint. Record the floor in
                     `bench-instruments` and stop setting CC below it.

      other       ............................................

  CROSS-CHECK using 1B
  --------------------
      With the REAL current from 1A, does the pair (current, Vf) sit on
      the curve drawn by the two CV points, 2.322 V at 0.69 mA and
      2.454 V at 2.57 mA?

      [ ] yes  -> one LED explains everything; the supply is the culprit

TEST 2: GREEN FORWARD VOLTAGE AT THE LABEL'S OWN CONDITION
===========================================================================

  TOP component: the 120 ohm resistor.
  SUPPLY: CV mode, 5.00 V, current limit 50 mA.

  THIS METHOD DOES NOT CARE WHETHER CC IS HONEST. The resistor sets the
  current and the drop across it tells you what you actually got. That is
  the whole reason for using it rather than CC.

  2R  Measure the 120 ohm OUT OF CIRCUIT, clips not fingers.
                                            R = ............ ohm

  --- RGB GREEN ---------------------------------------------------------

  2A  Fluke DC V, probes B to C, across the die.
                                            Vf = ............ V
  2B  Fluke DC V, probes A to B, across the resistor.
                                         drop = ............ V
                                      current = drop / R
                                              = ............ mA

  --- BICOLOUR GREEN ----------------------------------------------------

  2C  Fluke DC V, probes B to C, across the die.
                                            Vf = ............ V
  2D  Fluke DC V, probes A to B, across the resistor.
                                         drop = ............ V
                                      current = drop / R
                                              = ............ mA

  VERDICT, per die
  ----------------
      3.0-3.2 V    LABEL IS RIGHT. The die is InGaN. The 2026-09-23
                   correction stands and nothing further is needed.

      ~2.5-2.7 V   LABEL IS WRONG. The die is AlGaInP. The 2026-09-22
                   claim was right for the wrong reason, which is NOT the
                   same as having been right: the reasoning that produced
                   it was invalid either way and stays withdrawn.

      other        ............................................

  SANITY: the current from 2B/2D should land somewhere in 16-21 mA. If it
  is far outside that, something is wrong with the rig before the reading
  means anything. A common cause is a second cathode not left floating.
